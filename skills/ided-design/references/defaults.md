# Defaults to name before you design

You cannot avoid a pattern you have not named. Before you commit to a direction, write down the
page this category always produces and its predictable opposite: both are the rut. Then read
your plan against the lists below. Any of these is legitimate when the brief asks for it; the
problem is reaching for one because it is what comes out when nothing was decided.

## What generated design clusters around

- A warm cream or off-white ground, a high-contrast serif or heavy grotesque headline, and one
  warm accent (terracotta, vermilion, clay).
- A near-black ground with a single acid or signal-orange accent.
- Broadsheet pastiche: hairline rules everywhere, dense columns, newspaper furniture on content
  that is not news.
- The card kit: content chopped into identical rounded boxes of equal weight, one radius and one
  soft shadow on everything.
- Template chrome that appears whatever the subject: a tracked-out uppercase label above every
  heading, meta strings joined with middle dots, "01 / 02 / 03" numbering on things that are not
  a sequence, an arrow appended to every link.
- One word or phrase in a headline picked out in the accent color or italics.
- The big number with a small label under it, three across.
- Everything centered on one axis, every frame the same layout.
- Gradient washes, glows and blobs as decoration.

## What ided's own scaffolding produces

The templates `ided new` writes are placeholders, and they have a look: a title and a muted
one-line subhead at the bottom left, the mark in the top-right corner. If a finished frame still
has that arrangement, it was not designed; it was filled in. The same goes for the starter
brand's footer (label plus frame number) on every slide: use chrome only where it helps the
reader find their place, which is rarely every frame of a short deck.

## Deck-specific ruts

- Title slide, agenda, three bullet slides, "thank you / questions?".
- A bullet list as the answer to every kind of content.
- Headlines that are topics ("Costs") instead of claims ("Rates fall 22% for members").
- Every slide the same layout at the same density.
- Section divider slides that say nothing.

## Document-specific ruts

- A cover page on a one-page document.
- Everything the same size so nothing has to be chosen.
- A decorative header band eating the top fifth of the page.
- Justified text with rivers, or centered paragraphs.

## Graphic-specific ruts

- Headline centered in an empty field, logo bottom-center.
- A stock-feeling phrase ("Big news!", "Introducing…", "We're excited to announce").
- Filling every corner.

## Copy ruts

Copy makes a design look as templated as layout does.

- Invented contact details: a plausible-looking web address or phone number that nobody gave you.
  ided rejects typed URLs, emails and phone numbers anyway; use `<Fact>` or a visible placeholder.
- Invented numbers, testimonials or logos. Use only facts you were given; if the piece needs a
  number you do not have, leave it out or ask.
- Superlatives doing the work of evidence ("revolutionary", "seamless", "powerful").
- Eyebrow labels that restate the heading below them.
- Headlines that could belong to any company. Read the headline with the name removed: if it
  still fits a competitor, it is not specific yet.
