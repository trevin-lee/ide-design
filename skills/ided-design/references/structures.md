# Structures

A structure is the shape of the whole piece: how the content is divided and in what order the
viewer meets it. Choose it before any styling. Structural sameness (every deck is title, three
bullets, cards, thank-you) is what makes work look templated even when every token is right.

Each entry says when it fits and the trap it falls into. The names match `scripts/roll.mjs`.
Check other projects' DESIGN.md files first: a workspace that used `argument-ladder` for its last
three decks needs a reason to use it a fourth time.

## Decks

**statement-sequence.** One sentence per slide, set large; the slide is the sentence. For
speaker-led talks where the words are the argument. *Trap:* sentences that are not worth the
size. If a line would not survive on a poster, it does not deserve a slide.

**argument-ladder.** Claim, evidence, implication, each on its own rung, the next slide
answering the question the last one raised. For persuasion with a skeptical but rational room.
*Trap:* evidence slides become bullet dumps; each rung gets one piece of evidence, shown.

**objection-answer.** Each slide takes one objection the audience already holds, states it in
their words, then answers it. For rooms that arrive doubting. *Trap:* straw men. Use the real
objection, phrased the way they would say it.

**ledger.** The story as one evolving document: a table, a tide chart, a balance sheet, a
schedule, revealed row by row across slides. For content that is really a comparison or an
accounting. *Trap:* shrinking type to fit the whole table; reveal it instead.

**before-after.** Alternating "what is" and "what could be" (Duarte's contrast), ending on the
new state. For change proposals. *Trap:* a cartoonish "before"; the present should look true.

**one-object.** A single artifact (a product, a map, a number, a photograph) examined from a
different angle on every slide. For focused launches and explanations. *Trap:* running out of
angles by slide four; plan the angles before building.

**map.** One base image or diagram that stays put while each slide highlights a different part.
For places, systems and processes. *Trap:* highlights that differ too little to notice.

**chapters.** Full-bleed section covers between dense content slides, the rhythm alternating
loud and quiet. For long decks read without a speaker. *Trap:* covers with nothing to say.

**timeline.** Time as the axis the whole deck moves along. Only for content that is genuinely a
sequence. *Trap:* numbering things that are not steps.

## Documents

**letter.** Written to one reader in the first person; the layout is a well-set page of prose
with one or two strong anchors. *Trap:* decorating it until it stops reading like a letter.

**timetable.** The schedule is the design: times, days and places set as a clear typographic
table, everything else subordinate. For classes, events, programs. *Trap:* burying the table
under an introduction nobody reads.

**poster-flyer.** One dominant element seen from across a room, then a compact information
block read up close. Two distances, two hierarchies. *Trap:* five things competing to be the
dominant one.

**report.** Summary first (the answer on page one), then the evidence in descending
importance. *Trap:* the summary written as a teaser instead of the answer.

**reference-card.** Modular, scannable, meant to be consulted rather than read: consistent
modules on a strict grid. *Trap:* modules of equal weight when the content is not equal.

**field-guide.** Entries with a fixed anatomy (name, image, facts, note) repeated with
discipline. *Trap:* breaking the anatomy for one entry.

**manifesto.** A small number of large statements with room around them. *Trap:* ten
statements; three is a manifesto, ten is a list.

## Graphics

**type-as-image.** The words are the picture: scale, cropping and placement do the work. For
announcements whose message is short. *Trap:* a headline in the middle of an empty field is not
type as image; the type must be composed.

**one-object.** A single subject, large, cropped with intent, with minimal text. *Trap:* a
small object floating in the middle.

**diagram.** The idea drawn as structure: a flow, a comparison, a cross-section. *Trap:*
diagram as decoration, with no information in it.

**document-fragment.** A piece of the subject's own world (a file, a receipt, a page of notes,
a ticket) staged as the image. Specific by construction. *Trap:* a fake-looking fragment; use
the real format and real content.

**split.** Two halves in tension: old and new, problem and answer, image and statement.
*Trap:* halves of equal weight when one should win.

**field-pattern.** A repeated element (a mark, a word, a shape) filling the frame, with one
deviation carrying the message. *Trap:* pattern with no deviation, which is wallpaper.

**quote.** A single voice, attributed. Only for real quotes from real people. *Trap:* an
invented testimonial.

## Web screens

**poster-hero.** The first viewport treated as a poster: one image or one sentence, nothing
competing. *Trap:* the default big-headline-plus-two-buttons hero.

**long-read.** Editorial: a measure you can read, a clear typographic hierarchy, images at the
points they illustrate. *Trap:* card grids breaking up an argument.

**demo-first.** The product in action is the hero; the words explain what is being shown.
*Trap:* a screenshot shrunk into a device frame.

**index.** A directory, table or catalog as the page itself. *Trap:* turning rows into cards.

**manifesto.** Principles, set with room. *Trap:* principles that any company could sign.

**ledger.** Pricing, comparisons, specifications set as honest tables. *Trap:* a comparison
table rigged so every row wins.
