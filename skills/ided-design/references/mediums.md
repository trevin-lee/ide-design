# Mediums

## Decks (slides, 1920×1080)

- Ask or decide early: **speaker-led or read alone?** A speaker-led deck carries one claim per
  slide, large, with the evidence spoken; a read-alone deck carries the argument in writing and
  can be denser. It changes slide count, type scale and word budget.
- The headline of each slide is a sentence stating its point. The evidence (a number, an image,
  a diagram, a short list) sits below it.
- Budget: about three seconds to get each slide. When content does not fit, split the slide;
  never shrink the type to make it fit.
- Plan the arc on paper first: one line per slide, read in order. If the one-liners do not tell
  the story, no layout will.
- Rhythm: alternate density and quiet. Give the moment that matters most a slide to itself.
- Surfaces set the mood per section; decide which slides get the dark or brand surface, and
  why, as part of the color strategy.
- Chrome (logo, footer, slide number) only where it helps; a six-slide talk rarely needs numbers.

## Documents (pages, Letter/A4 at 2×)

- Two reading distances: a page is glanced at, then read. Design the glance (one dominant
  element, the answer first) and then the read (measure, rhythm, clear sections).
- `body` for running text, set to a readable measure; headings from the brand's heading styles.
- Every page of a multi-page document shares its furniture (running header or mark, page
  number with `FrameNumber`), and nothing else is repeated needlessly.
- A one-page document does not need a cover.
- Running text flows: put a report's or essay's body in one `<Page flow>` with its furniture in
  `chrome`, and let the pages fall where they fall. Pages with a design of their own (the cover,
  a chapter opener, a full-bleed figure, the back page) are separate files around it. Keep
  flowing content to paragraphs, headings and modest blocks; anything taller than a page fails
  the check.
- For printed pieces (flyers, posters), design for the distance they are read from; test with a
  screenshot at about 25%.

## Graphics (social and print sizes)

- Fewer words than you think: often one statement, one supporting line, the mark.
- Feed size is small and fast. Test at 25% scale: if the message does not land at thumbnail
  size, it does not land.
- Keep essential content inside the margin; platforms crop.
- A graphic is often seen alongside a caption; the caption can carry details so the image does
  not have to.

## Web screens

- The first viewport is a poster: one image or one sentence that the subject owns.
- Write real content; the layout follows it. Sections do one job each.
- Keep a readable measure with fractional widths even on wide screens.
- Cards only for things that really are a set of comparable items.
